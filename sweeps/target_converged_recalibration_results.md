# target_num_converged recalibration: results (job 545344)

Sweep A reused `target_num_converged={2,6}` from an old, pre-`n_simul`-fix calibration without re-deriving it on the corrected code (flagged at the time, see `sweeps/STATUS.md`). This sweep reruns both sequential selectors at `members_per_group=8` over `target_num_converged in {1,2,3,4,6,8,10,12,16,24}` (280 tasks, all completed, 0 failures), for asced48 and asced384, against the `full_parallel` (no early stopping) baseline. `target_errors=200`, `max_transmissions=2e6` per point (same as Sweep A).

Low-sample FER points (<50 frame errors observed) are prefixed `~` -- read those as directional only, not precise.

## Key findings

- **asced48**: FER is essentially insensitive to `target_num_converged` across the whole swept range {1..24} for both selectors -- even `target_num_converged=1` (stop after the very first converged path) matches `full_parallel` within noise at every SNR. Effort/latency savings are largest at low target values and shrink monotonically back toward the `full_parallel` baseline as target increases past ~10 (the natural average-converged-path count at high SNR). **`target_num_converged=1` or `2` looks close to optimal for asced48** -- old Sweep A's `6` was unnecessarily conservative (far higher effort/latency than `1`/`2` for no measurable FER benefit).

- **asced384 is different and this matters**: `target_num_converged=1` shows a clear, well-sampled (300+ frame errors, not a low-sample artifact) FER FLOOR at moderate-to-high SNR -- e.g. fixed_sequential @ 4.0dB: FER=0.0163 at target=1 vs. full_parallel's ~1.1e-05 (~1500x worse); @ 3.0dB: 0.026 vs. 0.00097 (~27x worse). `target=2` is much better but still measurably worse than full_parallel at high SNR (@ 4.0dB: 2.3e-04 vs ~1.1e-05, ~21x worse; 332 frame errors, well-sampled). **`target=3` is roughly where asced384 recovers to matching full_parallel within noise**, and `target>=4` is clearly safe. **This means Sweep A's asced384 results at `target_num_converged=2` likely understate FER performance relative to full_parallel/large-target configurations at high SNR -- flag this if that comparison is used anywhere (e.g. the published plot).**

- Intuition: with only 48 total paths, even 1-2 converged candidates are enough to reliably include the true codeword; with 384 paths, stopping after just 1-2 converged candidates risks locking in a spurious-but-valid low-weight codeword before the genuinely correct one (among the larger pool) has had a chance to converge too -- a real, ensemble-size-dependent effect, not a code-family artifact.

- Recommendation: recalibrate to **asced48: target_num_converged=2** (small safety margin over the apparently-fine target=1, negligible extra cost), **asced384: target_num_converged=4** (comfortably past the target=3 recovery point, still far cheaper than full_parallel or the old target=6). Both selectors (fixed_sequential, syndrome_sequential) behave similarly, no strong reason to prefer one from this sweep alone.
